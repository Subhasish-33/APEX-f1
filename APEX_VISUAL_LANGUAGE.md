# APEX-F1 Visual Identity System
# Tier 5 / Phase 3 — Cinematic Editorial Experience

## 1. Emotional Tone
APEX feels **expensive, precise, editorial, composed, and calm**.
It is the ultimate motorsport intelligence platform. It never screams for attention. It earns attention through mechanical precision and cinematic restraint.

## 2. Visual Rhythm & Density Philosophy
- **Asymmetrical Layout Rhythm**: Do not force everything into equal boxes. Allow telemetry tables to span 3 columns while a cinematic hero image takes 9 columns.
- **Aggressive Whitespace Discipline**: Negative space is a design element, not "empty space". It isolates data points so they feel important.
- **Editorial Density Balancing**: Dense telemetry matrices must be offset by large, quiet photographic areas. 

## 3. Atmospheric Discipline
- Atmosphere is **subtle**. 
- Allowed: Cinematic grain, soft linear gradients (low opacity), restrained background glows (team colors), carbon textures.
- Not Allowed: Bright neon borders, cyberpunk RGB glitch effects, holographic overload. It is a luxury instrument, not an arcade game.

## 4. Typography Identity (System V3)
Typography is our primary cinematic tool.
- **Oversized Translucent Numerals**: Used for race positions and car numbers. They sit in the background, creating depth.
- **Telemetry Monospace**: Monospace fonts are used STRICTLY for data (lap times, sector deltas, tire ages). Do not use monospace for prose.
- **Optical Scaling**: Headers track tight. Small telemetry tracks wide for legibility.
- **Asymmetrical Hierarchy**: H1s should not just be "bigger". They should have distinct weight and placement (e.g., pushed to the bottom left of a hero block).

## 5. Composition System
- **Layered Depth**: Z-index is used to build presence. The background sets the team mood, the typography sets the context, the data sits on top.
- **Focal-Point Aware Crops**: All images use the backend's focal point data (`objectPosition`) so the subject's eyes or the car's nose are always perfectly aligned.
- **Telemetry-Safe Zones**: Images have "safe zones" where typography can sit without clashing with the focal point.
